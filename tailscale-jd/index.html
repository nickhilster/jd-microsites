The operator's seat

Three things your other candidates cannot say.

Aperture's buyers are CISOs, platform engineers and IT admins. I have sat in their chair — from inside my own fleet.

01

Work from phone

People talk about work from home; I work from phone. Agent direction, draft review, approvals — the governance moments happen wherever I am. That only works because identity carries the access. It is the Aperture thesis, lived from the user's side.

02

The daemon finding

Security research I commissioned on my own fleet found that any local process could read the full tailnet map through the daemon's socket — and ranked isolating the agent from the host daemon as the fix. I have had your customers' hardest argument, from their side of the table.

03

Policy, enforced by design

Inside a regulated enterprise (Rothmans, Benson and Hedges), the AI agent I designed ran on one standard — AI drafts, human reviews, human approves — and it became the pattern for AI output there. Governance people can actually adopt, because it is built into the workflow.
