How I work

Three principles, learned at the headset.

Product ownership for a contact center is not a desk discipline. It is a floor discipline with a roadmap attached.

01

Run the floor before you roadmap it.

Advisor workflows are the product. Every prioritization call I make has to survive contact with the person wearing the headset and the customer on the line — because I have been the person who lives with those calls.

02

Prioritize by friction, not by volume.

The loudest queue is rarely the costliest one. I read service performance data — NPS, turnaround, repeat contacts — to find where customers and advisors actually bleed time, and the backlog follows the friction.

03

AI earns its place. Humans keep the keys.

AI servicing ships when it measurably helps, grounded in real sources, with consequential actions held for a human's approval. That is not caution. In a regulated business, it is how the technology survives its first audit.

Method

How a backlog earns its order.

ObserveSit with advisors and customers; watch the journey, not the dashboard.
BaselineNPS, turnaround, repeat contacts — the numbers before anyone touches anything.
SpecifyEpics, stories and acceptance criteria a delivery team can actually build to.
VerifyValidate delivered functionality against the criteria, on the floor, with advisors.
MeasureDid the friction move? Report it either way, and re-prioritize.

First 90 days

A pod that ships what the floor needs.

Days 1–30

Listen and baseline

Shadow advisors across channels, map the top customer journeys end to end, and baseline the service metrics the pod will be judged by. Meet every stakeholder group with questions, not a roadmap.

Days 31–60

Rebuild the backlog around friction

Re-prioritize the backlog against the friction evidence: customer value, operational benefit, risk and delivery capacity, in that order of argument. Scope the first AI-enabled servicing improvement with its human-review controls designed in from the start.

Days 61–90

Ship, validate, report

Deliver the first capability into advisors' hands, validate it against its acceptance criteria on live contacts, and report the metric movement — or the miss — plainly, with the next prioritization call already argued.
