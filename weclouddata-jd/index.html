How I teach

Three principles, learned on the floor.

A bootcamp is a deployment with students. The habits that make deployments work are the habits that make teaching work.

01

Build first, lecture second.

Every concept lands on a working system the same day it is introduced. Students meet RAG by querying a live pipeline, and agents by approving — and refusing — real agent actions.

02

Narrate the work.

Say what you are about to do, and why, before you do it. Narration is how a learner stays oriented inside unfamiliar machinery — and how a deployed engineer keeps a client's trust.

03

Adapt to the person in front of you.

An explanation is not finished when it is correct; it is finished when the learner can act on it. I have taught new hires and senior citizens in the same week. Range is the job.

The six weeks

How I would run the bootcamp.

Weeks 1–2

Foundations on live systems

Lectures and labs anchored to working deployments from day one: LLM application patterns, prompt engineering, RAG pipelines and agent basics, each ending in something a student built and can break safely.

Weeks 3–4

The implementation case study

Mentored end-to-end builds: architecture reviews, troubleshooting sessions, evaluation checkpoints, and the governance pass — security, guardrails, human-in-the-loop — before anything is called done.

Weeks 5–6

The client capstone

Discovery, requirements and delivery for a real client, with deployment-readiness reviews and executive-presentation coaching. Students leave with a system, and the judgment to defend it.
