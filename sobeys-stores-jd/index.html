What this role actually is

Ownership of the store-technology target state: the Next Gen POS strategy and execution roadmap, a delivery organization of internal builders and external partners, vendor accountability across the POS and payments ecosystem, a merchant acquirer transition on committed timelines, continuous enhancement of electronic shelf labels — and, underneath all of it, the stability of the core store systems more than 1,600 stores trade on today. It rewards a director who can hold the future-state architecture and Saturday morning in the same head.

01

Start where the technology lands.

Requirements shaped with operations, merchandising and marketing are necessary — and insufficient. The roadmap has to survive contact with a real store: its people, its peak hours, its service bay. Design from the floor up, then govern from the program down.

02

Make delivery a discipline, not an event.

A disciplined delivery model tied to program governance: baselines, dependency maps, risk registers, and trade-offs surfaced to executives early — scope, cost, timeline, operational impact — while they are still choices, not surprises.

03

Pilot in real stores. Scale what earns it.

New store technology proves itself in a working store or it does not ship at scale. Pilot, measure against the operation it changed, stabilize — then roll out on a release discipline the network can absorb.

First 90 days

DAYS 1–30

Stores & estate baseline.

Time in stores with operations and the teams who keep today’s POS, payments and ESL systems standing. Baseline the estate: integrations, dependencies, vendor commitments, operational risks — and the acquirer transition’s real position against its timelines.

DAYS 31–60

Roadmap & delivery model.

Target-state architecture and execution roadmap locked to the POS program’s governance cadence. Vendor accountability made explicit across the POS and payments ecosystem; executive reporting that surfaces scope, cost, timeline and operational trade-offs early, in writing.

DAYS 61–90

Pilot discipline.

Prove the delivery model in real stores: pilot scope, success measures tied to store operations, stabilization criteria before scale. The existing core systems stay stable throughout — enhancement without disruption is the first deliverable.
