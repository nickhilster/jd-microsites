What this role actually is

The owner of the measurement framework — satisfaction, first-contact resolution, customer effort, complaint trends — and of the system that turns those signals into governed, prioritized change: journey management, insight-to-action, and digital and AI adoption across Customer Operations, reported at executive level to the VP, Customer Operations. 01

Measure what the customer feels, then act on it in the open.

Satisfaction, resolution, effort and complaints are one framework, not four reports. The dashboard earns its place only when a coaching conversation, a quality standard or a workflow changes because of it.

02

Journeys are governed, not just mapped.

A journey map that ends in a workshop is decoration. Journey management means named owners, prioritized pain points, business cases, and outcomes measured after the fix ships — sustained, not announced.

03

Digital and AI exist to remove effort.

Self-serve, automation and AI assistance are adopted where they measurably cut customer effort and protect first-contact quality — with human judgment governing the moments where trust is decided.

First 90 days

DAYS 1–30

Listen & baseline.

Sit with Customer Operations Delivery and the listening posts already in place. Baseline the measurement framework as it stands — satisfaction, resolution, effort, complaint trends by journey — and publish the first honest picture to the VP, Customer Operations.

DAYS 31–60

Prioritize & govern.

Stand up the journey-management cadence: named journey owners, a prioritization framework, and the first portfolio of improvement initiatives — each with a business case connecting customer outcomes to operational and financial value.

DAYS 61–90

Ship & measure.

First digital / self-serve / AI improvements in customers’ hands, with before-and-after measurement on the framework’s own indicators — and the multi-year CX strategy drafted from evidence gathered, not inherited assumptions.
