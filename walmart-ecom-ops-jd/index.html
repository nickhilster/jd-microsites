What this role actually is

The design authority for how Walmart Canada’s stores absorb eCommerce demand: OMNI space and layout, fulfillment capacity, speed and cost — expressed as strategic roadmaps, scalable standards, and business cases that Store Operations, Format Development, eCommerce and Finance can all build from. It rewards an operator who starts from how the work actually runs on the floor, and ends with numbers an executive can sign.

01

Design the flow before the floor plan.

A layout is a frozen workflow. Intake, adjacencies, hand-offs and equipment placement decide capacity long before a wall moves — so the operation gets designed first, and the space follows it.

02

Capacity, speed and cost are one problem.

Trade them against each other and all three lose. Redesign the process and the same footprint produces more, faster, for less — that is the result I bring receipts for.

03

Standards that scale are written from the floor.

A layout standard survives contact with four hundred stores only if it was earned in real ones: observed on the floor, measured on dashboards, then written so the next location starts from evidence.

First 90 days

DAYS 1–30

Walk the operation.

Time in the highest-volume OMNI stores, on the floor with associates and Store Operations: how demand actually arrives, where process, equipment and space pinch, what the dashboards say versus what the floor shows. Baseline capacity, speed and cost as they run today.

DAYS 31–60

Map capacity, cost the redesigns.

A capacity map across demand, process, equipment and space, built with Format Development, eCommerce and Finance — and the first redesign candidates expressed as business cases with expected capacity, speed and cost effects.

DAYS 61–90

Pilot, measure, standardize.

Run the first layout/flow pilots in live stores, measure against the baseline, and draft the scalable standards the network can adopt — with the roadmap for the next wave ready for executive review.
