Enhancements fail in the handoff, not in the code.

The Home Depot Canada Business Analyst gathers and documents the requirements behind website, mobile app and retail channel enhancements, and works as a key contact between end users, business units, IT and Product Management. That handoff is where enhancements succeed or fail — and it is the work below: business functions and processes defined from the floor up, redesigned against measured outcomes, specified so both sides build from the same page, and accepted before rollout.

What this role actually is

Read closely, this is a translation role with a paper trail: turn what the business needs and what end users actually do into requirements, functional specifications and test cases that IT can build against — then write the procedures and training that make the change hold after launch. It rewards an analyst who has stood on every side of the handoff: the retail floor where processes land, the enterprise platform team where requirements are negotiated, and the builder’s desk where specifications become shipped systems. That combination is rare because most analysts only ever sit in one chair. I have sat in all three.

01

Gather from the people who do the work.

Information gathering starts with end users, not with the request form. Document current operational procedures as they actually happen — the workarounds are requirements too.

02

Specify so both sides build from it.

Business Requirements Docs, use cases, process diagrams and functional specifications that end users and IT read the same way — with changes tracked and design rulings recorded, not remembered.

03

Acceptance before rollout.

Test cases and user acceptance testing are defined up front, and the end-user procedures and training are written before go-live — so the enhancement holds after the launch meeting ends.

An enhancement succeeds or fails in the handoff — between what the business meant and what the system got. The analyst’s job is to make the handoff hold.

The six-month term, in three moves

MOVE 1 · WEEKS 1–3

Make the current state legible.

Document the enhancement intake as it actually flows, the operational procedures end users actually follow, and the documentation set Product Management decides from — a baseline the whole team can point at.

MOVE 2 · FIRST CYCLE

Run one enhancement end to end.

Requirements, functional specifications, test case definition, user acceptance testing, procedures and training — one live enhancement carried through the full cycle, with end users, IT and Product Management in the same loop.

MOVE 3 · REST OF TERM

Leave the template behind.

Codify the worked cycle as the documentation standard — BRD and use-case shapes, change tracking, acceptance checklists — so the pattern outlives the contract and the team that stays owns it.
